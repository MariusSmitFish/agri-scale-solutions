Services

What we build

A farm website has one job: help the right person understand what you offer, where you are, and what to do next. These are the pieces that do that.

  1. 01

    Websites for working farms

    A clear home for the place itself. Who you are, what you raise or grow, when you are open, and how to buy, visit, or join a share. Written the way you would explain it at the gate, and laid out for someone reading on a phone in the car park.

  2. 02

    Websites for agricultural businesses

    Suppliers, contractors, nurseries, co-ops, and advisors need to look established and make a serious enquiry easy. We build sites that explain the work, show the area you cover, and collect the details you actually need.

  3. 03

    Pages that answer the weekly questions

    CSA details, u-pick rules, farm-shop hours, barn weddings, school visits. The things you repeat all season, written once, in the place people already look.

  4. 04

    Found in your own county

    Page titles, descriptions, and local wording so a search for what you sell has a fair chance of showing your name. We also tidy your Google Business Profile and check the map pin is actually on the farm.

  5. 05

    Looked after after launch

    Seasons move faster than most websites. We can change what is in season, add a product, or show you the few edits you will really want to make yourself.

Process

Four steps. No mystery.

You do not need a brand deck or a finished brief. You need half an hour and the facts about the business.

  1. 01

    A short conversation

    Tell us about the land or the business, the customers, and what the site should do. Half an hour is usually enough.

  2. 02

    Words, then design

    We draft the pages in plain language and show you the design on a phone before anything is public.

  3. 03

    You correct the facts

    Hours, prices, names, photos. You mark what is wrong. We change it. Nothing goes live until you say so.

  4. 04

    Launch, then a handover

    The site goes up, we walk you through it, and you know who to call when the pumpkin patch opens.

Working together

A plain way of working.

Most farm websites fail quietly. They are slow on a phone, vague about what is actually for sale, and frozen in last year’s season. We build the opposite: a small, sharp site you can trust in the middle of harvest.

We do not publish one price, because a single-page farm stand and a multi-page dealer site are different jobs. After we talk, you get a written quote with what is included and how long it will take.

  • A design made for this business, not a template with the logo swapped
  • Pages that load quickly, including on a slow rural connection
  • A layout that is comfortable on a phone
  • A contact form that arrives in your inbox
  • Map, hours, and directions
  • Room for the photos you already have
  • Basic setup so Google can read the site
  • A short walkthrough when we launch

Questions

Before you write to us

Do I have to write everything?

No. We draft the pages from a conversation. You correct anything that does not sound like you, or that gets a fact wrong. If you already have words you like, we will use them.

What if my photos are just phone pictures?

Phone photos of the real place are better than polished pictures of someone else’s farm. We will help you choose a handful. If a photographer is worth it, we will say so.

I already have a website.

We can replace it, or keep the parts that work — including an address customers already know. Tell us what feels broken and we will start there.

How long does a typical farm site take?

A straightforward farm site is often two to four weeks once we have the facts and photos. A larger agricultural business takes longer. The quote includes the timeline.

Will I be stuck paying you forever?

No. The site is yours. Hosting can sit on an account in your name. If you stop working with us, you take the website with you. Upkeep after launch is optional, and we will price it separately.
